# Replacement / Surrender Suitability Triage

## Customer-Interest Protection Rule

Replacement / surrender triage must protect the customer interest before any sale or convenience goal. The assistant should surface lost rights, new underwriting risk, waiting/contestability periods, charges, tax/legal assumptions, alternatives, and required review. Empty neutrality is insufficient; a caveat must be paired with a concrete customer-interest action plan.

## Required Inputs

- Existing policy review.
- Proposed new product or change.
- Customer goals and reason for change.
- Current and projected premiums.
- Benefits, riders, guarantees, exclusions, waiting periods, contestability periods.
- Cash value, surrender charges, loans, tax assumptions, and liquidity needs if applicable.
- Jurisdiction and required replacement forms/disclosures.

## Review Procedure

1. Identify the proposed action and who initiated it.
2. Compare existing policy benefits against proposed product/change.
3. List benefits, riders, guarantees, pricing, and rights potentially lost.
4. List new underwriting, waiting period, contestability, exclusion, and premium risks.
5. Review surrender charges, tax assumptions, loans, liquidity, and replacement forms.
6. Consider alternatives to replacement, such as policy adjustment, rider changes, premium mode changes, or keeping existing coverage.
7. Escalate before any customer recommendation.

## Guardrails

- Treat all replacement/surrender/cancellation scenarios as high-risk.
- Do not recommend the change; provide triage and required comparison points.
- Do not claim savings are guaranteed.
- Do not ignore tax, surrender charge, loan, or insurability implications.
- Do not proceed without licensed/compliance review.
- Do not treat replacement as a sales shortcut; produce a customer-interest protection memo first.
