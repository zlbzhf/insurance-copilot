# Claims Triage

Use this workflow when the user asks about a possible claim, claim status, claim documentation, denial, appeal, late notice, limitation period, or coverage question. This workflow helps organize information and develop the strongest good-faith claim-support position; it does not decide claims.

## Boundary

Do not state that a claim is covered, payable, denied correctly, or guaranteed. Claims decisions belong to the carrier/claims administrator and applicable review/appeal process.

Customer-first claims support means do not stop at `the carrier decides`. Develop the strongest good-faith claim-support position the facts and policy can support, identify evidence to gather, preserve deadlines, and escalate review paths. Do not invent facts, alter documents, give unauthorized legal advice, or guarantee outcomes.

## Required Inputs

- Policy source and relevant coverage section if available.
- Loss/event date and high-level event description.
- Claim status, if any.
- Carrier claim instructions or correspondence.
- Deadlines and required documents.
- Jurisdiction if relevant.

## Review Procedure

1. Identify the type of claim or question.
2. Extract known facts and documents received.
3. Identify missing documents and deadlines.
4. Separate policy-language facts from assumptions.
5. Build a **claim advocacy memo**: favorable facts, policy hooks, exclusions/conditions to verify, missing evidence, deadlines, complaint/review/appeal routes, and customer-safe next steps.
6. For late notice or apparent time-bar issues, analyze the timeline carefully, including when the customer knew or should have known they had coverage, a loss, a right to claim, or a reason to contact the carrier. Mark jurisdiction-specific limitation arguments `[verify with claims/legal/compliance]`.
7. Draft customer-safe service language and an internal client-interest action plan.
8. Escalate complaints, denials, deadlines, vulnerable customers, legal threats, suspected fraud, or unresolved limitation-period questions.

## Guardrails

- Do not guarantee claims payout or coverage.
- Do not advise customer to alter facts or documentation.
- Do not provide legal advice about disputes or appeals.
- Mark coverage interpretations `[verify with carrier/claims specialist]` unless authoritative source and licensed review are provided.
- Escalate denials, complaints, deadlines, suspected fraud, or legal threats.
