## When This Applies

Use this page when an agent needs a public, source-backed orientation for helping a customer prepare an AIA Hong Kong claim. It is an internal knowledge support page for `[[aia]]`, not a final claims decision and not a substitute for policy contract terms, claim forms, or AIA claims correspondence.

## Required Documents

Public AIA guidance indicates that claim documents should be prepared before submission. The FAQ summary highlights common document themes:

- claim form and supporting documents for the relevant claim type;
- valid medical receipts showing patient name, consultation date, diagnosis, charge breakdown, and provider stamp or doctor signature;
- compensation breakdown if another insurer or party has already reimbursed part of the expense;
- claim-type-specific hospital, medical, death, accident, disability, critical illness, travel, or other supporting materials `[verify]` against the current official source.

## Submission Steps

- E-Claim: AIA describes AIA+ submission for selected Accident, Medical, Travel, and Designated Dining claims `[verify current eligibility]`.
- Paper claim: AIA describes paper submission for all claim types, including through an AIA financial planner, broker, independent financial adviser, or directly to AIA `[verify current channels]`.
- General public process: prepare claim documents, submit the claim application, receive notification of the claim result, then receive claim payment if approved and payable.
- Agent use: convert the public process into a customer evidence checklist and review route; do not file the claim or submit documents unless the user separately authorizes a reviewed side effect.

## Deadlines / Time Windows

- The public FAQ states that claim form and supporting documents generally should be submitted within 90 days of the relevant accident, day surgery date, hospital discharge date, or death of the insured.
- If submission is after the 90-day window, the FAQ states that a reason should be provided for consideration.
- AIA publishes claim-promise statistics such as typical first pending notice/result timing and FPS payment speed; treat these as public service metrics, not an outcome promise for a specific case `[verify]`.

## What Not To Promise

- This page is not a final claims decision.
- Do not state that coverage exists, claim documents are complete, a claim will be approved, or payment will occur by a fixed date.
- Do not say that a receipt, diagnosis, hospital document, death document, or other evidence is sufficient without checking the current official source and claim correspondence.
- Do not use a neutral caveat alone. Customer-first service should add evidence requests, deadlines, review owner, and customer-safe next steps.
